# Behavioral Interview Preparation

Behavioral interviews assess how you've handled past situations. The premise: past behavior predicts future behavior. Interviewers at top tech companies (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) use behavioral questions to assess culture fit, leadership principles, and soft skills.

---

## What Companies Look For

**Amazon**: Leadership Principles (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Bias for Action, Deliver Results, etc.). Expect at least 50% of the interview to be behavioral.

**Google**: Googleyness (collaboration, intellectual humility, initiative), Leadership, Role-Related Knowledge.

**Meta**: Move Fast, Be Direct, Build Awesome Things, Focus on Long-Term Impact.

**General traits all companies assess:**
- Communication clarity
- Ownership mentality (do you take responsibility or blame others?)
- Teamwork and conflict resolution
- Growth mindset (do you learn from failure?)
- Impact orientation (do your actions produce measurable results?)

---

## The STAR Method

STAR is a framework for structuring behavioral answers. Use it for every question.

### Template

**S — Situation**
Set the context. Where were you working? What was the project? What were the circumstances?
- Keep this brief (2–3 sentences)
- Provide enough context for the interviewer to understand the environment

**T — Task**
What was your specific responsibility or challenge?
- What were you asked to do?
- What problem needed to be solved?
- What was at stake?

**A — Action**
What did YOU specifically do? This is the most important part.
- Use "I", not "we" — interviewers want to know what YOU contributed
- Be specific about decisions you made and why
- Describe 2–3 key actions with reasoning

**R — Result**
What happened? Be specific and quantify where possible.
- Use metrics: "reduced latency by 40%", "shipped 2 weeks early", "increased revenue by $500K"
- If the project failed, describe what you learned
- Always end with impact

### Common STAR Anti-Patterns

- **Too much S/T, not enough A**: Don't spend 4 minutes on background and 30 seconds on what you did
- **Using "we" instead of "I"**: Interviewers can't assess your contribution
- **No result**: Trailing off without stating what happened
- **Vague actions**: "I worked hard to fix the problem" — what specifically did you do?
- **No learning**: For failure questions, always extract a lesson

### Length

Aim for 3–4 minutes per answer. Practice telling your stories so you can hit that window consistently.

## Preparing Your Stories

Before the interview, prepare 8–10 strong STAR stories from your experience. Good stories can answer multiple questions:

- A major incident you debugged → works for "challenging problem", "production issue", "failure"
- A process improvement → works for "improved a process", "went above and beyond", "impact"
- A technical disagreement → works for "disagreement", "convince someone", "difficult stakeholder"

Think about these areas:
- Your most impactful project
- A failure or mistake
- A technical disagreement
- A time you led or mentored
- A time you had to learn something quickly

## Tips for Delivery

1. **Tell stories, not summaries**: "The service was failing silently — alerts were firing at 3am but the root cause wasn't obvious..." pulls the interviewer in.

2. **Make the stakes clear**: Why did this matter? What would have happened if you hadn't acted?

3. **Show your thinking**: "I considered two options — revert the deploy immediately vs investigate live. I chose to revert first because customer impact outweighed diagnosis time."

4. **Be specific about your role**: "I was the tech lead for the 3-person sub-team responsible for the data migration."

5. **Practice out loud**: Reading your stories in your head feels different than saying them. Practice until you can tell each story naturally in 3–4 minutes.

6. **Avoid company bashing**: If you describe a conflict, focus on the situation and actions, not on how wrong the other person was.

7. **Close with learning**: Even positive stories benefit from "and what I'd do differently is..."
