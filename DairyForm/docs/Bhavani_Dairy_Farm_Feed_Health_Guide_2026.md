## 6. BEST MODEL RECOMMENDATION

### **RECOMMENDED: Model B — Own Napier + Purchased Concentrate**

**Reasons specific to Pydi Bhimavaram, Srikakulam:**

1. **Climate is ideal for Napier** — Srikakulam's humid subtropical climate with 1,100–1,200 mm rainfall supports Napier grass year-round. CO-4 variety survives even during short dry spells.

2. **Land availability** — Gara mandal has canal irrigation from Vamsadhara and Nagavali projects; 4 acres of leased agricultural land near the farm is readily available at ₹8,000–15,000/year/acre (total ₹60,000/year lease + ₹60,000 one-time planting = payback in Year 1).

3. **₹5.1 lakh annual saving** — At a 13% bank loan rate on ₹75,000 capital, the cost is negligible compared to savings.

4. **Rice bran advantage** — Srikakulam is a major paddy-milling district; deoiled rice bran is cheap and consistently available — ideal for home-mixing concentrates at ₹28.70/kg vs. buying ready-made pellets at ₹35–42/kg.

5. **Simplest operation** — Unlike silage (Model C), Napier only needs a simple harvest every 45–60 days with a sickle or chaff cutter. No pit management.

### Action Plan to Start Model B
1. Identify 4 acres leased land within 3 km of farm (canal-irrigated preferred)
2. Purchase Napier CO-4 slips from KVK Srikakulam (subsidised) or neighbouring farms
3. Plant October–November (post-monsoon, best establishment)
4. First harvest in 75–90 days; subsequent harvests every 45 days
5. Start buying home-mixed concentrate ingredients from local rice mills + oil cake dealers

## 10. SEASONAL HEALTH MANAGEMENT

### SUMMER (March–June) — Heat Stress Prevention

Srikakulam temperatures reach 40–44°C in April–May. Murrah buffaloes suffer above 32°C.

**Management actions:**
- **Cooling:** Run fans/foggers 6–10 AM and 2–6 PM; mud wallowing pond essential
- **Water:** Provide **100 litres of cool water** per animal per day; refresh 3× daily
- **Feed timing:** 70% of daily feed in early morning (5:30–9 AM) and evening (6–8 PM)
- **Electrolytes:** ORS-Vet / Electral Vet — 50–100 g/day in water during >38°C days
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** 100–150 g/day in feed (prevents acidosis + reduces heat stress)
- **Vitamin C:** 2 g IV/IM every 15 days during peak heat (April–May)
- **Reduce concentrate:** Decrease by 0.5–1 kg/day if animal refuses feed during peak heat

**Warning signs of heat stress:**
- Panting (>80 breaths/min); standing in water; off feed; milk drop >15%
- **Emergency:** Hose down with cold water; move to shade; call vet if rectal temp >41°C

---

### MONSOON (July–September) — Disease Peak Season

Srikakulam receives 1,100–1,200 mm rain; coastal humidity creates high disease pressure.

**Management actions:**
- **FMD booster** — critical in July (Srikakulam has seasonal FMD outbreaks)
- **Foot care:** Weekly hoof check; copper sulphate footbath (2%) 3× per week for all animals
- **Deworming** (August) — worm egg counts peak post-monsoon
- **Mastitis watch:** Increase CMT test to **twice weekly**; humidity increases Staph aureus
- **HS alert:** Any sudden fever (41°C+) + throat swelling + breathing difficulty → call vet IMMEDIATELY (HS kills in 24–48 hrs)
- **Shed drainage:** Drain floor twice daily; dry bedding prevents leptospirosis and foot rot
- **Antifungal feed treatment:** Mix boric acid 100 g/tonne or Mycosorb into feed (prevents mycotoxin in wet stored fodder)
- **Silage making window:** July–August for surplus maize/sorghum

---

### POST-MONSOON (October–November) — Recovery & Breeding

- **HS + BQ booster** (October)
- **Deworming** Round 3 (October)
- **Body condition scoring:** Target BCS 3.0–3.5 before winter; flush non-productive animals
- **Breeding/AI focus:** Peak conception rates October–December (natural estrus peak)
- Synchronise AI programme: Ovsynch protocol available at KVK Srikakulam

---

### WINTER (December–February) — Nutritional Support

- **Vitamin ADE injection** (January): 10 mL IM — prevents night blindness, boosts calf immunity
- **Mineral bolus** (January + December): 1 bolus/animal — 3-month sustained trace mineral release
- **Liver tonic course** (January): Livol / Amprolium — 50 mL/day × 14 days
- **Increase concentrate** by 0.5 kg/day in December–January (cold increases energy demand)
- **Bypass fat** (Megalac/RuFat): 100 g/day/lactating animal (supports milk fat in cooler months)
- **Dry cow therapy (DCT):** At drying off — apply intramammary antibiotic (Cepravin / Orbenin) in all 4 quarters to prevent dry-period mastitis

## 11. COMMON DISEASES — COASTAL AP BUFFALOES

| Disease | Season | Key Signs | First Aid | Prevention |
|---------|--------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **FMD** | Year-round; peak Oct–Dec | Blisters on feet/mouth, drooling, lameness, milk drop 80% | Isolate; disinfect shed; antiseptic on blisters; supportive care | Vaccine every 6 months (March + July) |
| **Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS)** | Pre-monsoon/monsoon | High fever 41°C+, throat swelling, loud breathing, sudden death | Oxytetracycline 10 mg/kg IV immediately; call vet | HS vaccine (May + October) |
| **Mastitis** | Year-round; peak monsoon | Hard swollen quarter, clots in milk, CMT positive, fever | Strip affected quarter; Amoxicillin-Cloxacillin intramammary; systemic antibiotics | CMT twice/week; proper milking hygiene; teat dipping post-milking |
| **Milk Fever (Hypocalcaemia)** | Within 48 hrs of calving | Cannot stand, cold ears/nose, low temp 37°C, tetany | IV Calcium Borogluconate 400 mL (slowly over 15 min); call vet | Calcium drench at calving; avoid high-calcium pre-calving diet |
| **Ketosis** | 2–6 weeks post-calving | Sweet-smelling breath, off feed, low milk, nervous signs | Propylene glycol 300 mL oral 2×/day; IV Dextrose 500 mL; B-complex injection | High-energy diet 3 weeks pre-calving; no over-conditioning |
| **Black Quarter (BQ)** | Monsoon | Sudden lameness, swollen thigh muscle (crepitating), high fever, death | Penicillin G 10,000 IU/kg IM (very high dose); call vet urgently | BQ vaccine (May + October) |
| **Tympany (Bloat)** | Year-round; peak spring | Grossly distended left flank, laboured breathing, discomfort | Stomach tube (trocar if severe); Turpentine 30 mL + linseed oil 500 mL oral; walk the animal | Avoid sudden change to lush green legume fodder; limit subabul feeding |
| **Retained Placenta** | Post-calving | Placenta not expelled >12 hours | Oxytocin 20 IU IM at 6 hours; manual removal by vet at 48–72 hrs only | Vitamin ADE in last month; selenium injection pre-calving; avoid selenium deficiency |
| **Theileriosis** | March–June (tick season) | High fever 41°C+, enlarged lymph nodes, pale mucosa, anaemia | **Buparvaquone (Butalex) 2.5 mg/kg IM — single dose (SPECIFIC treatment)** | Rakshavac-T vaccine (February); tick control with Deltamethrin; |
| **Foot Rot / Digital Dermatitis** | Monsoon | Lameness, foul-smelling lesions between hooves | 2% copper sulphate footbath; Oxytetracycline spray on lesion; Penicillin systemic | Weekly copper sulphate footbath during monsoon; dry bedding |
| **Mastitis (Dry period)** | Post-drying off | Detected at calving — swollen quarter | Systemic antibiotics; intramammary | Dry cow therapy (DCT) at every dry-off |

## 12. ANIMAL HEALTH TRACKING PROTOCOL

### Daily Morning Observation Checklist (per animal)

Record results in the Dairy Farm Management App → Health Records section.

| Check | Normal | Action if Abnormal |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| **Appetite** | Eating well within 15 min | Refusing feed = first sign of illness; check temp, record |
| **Rumination** | 6–8 hrs/day; 40–60 chews per cud | <4 hrs = digestive problem; reduce concentrate |
| **Body temperature** | 38.0–39.5°C | >40°C = fever; isolate, call vet |
| **Respiration** | 10–30 breaths/min | >60 = heat stress or pneumonia |
| **Milk yield** | Within 10% of usual | >15% drop = check for mastitis, fever, estrus |
| **Udder** | Soft, even, no heat/swelling | CMT test if any change |
| **Eyes/nose** | Clear, no discharge | Any discharge = respiratory/fever alert |
| **Faeces** | Formed, medium brown | Watery = dietary/infection; bloody = urgent; very dry = dehydration |
| **Gait** | Normal | Any limp = check all 4 feet for lesions |
| **Behaviour** | Social, calm | Isolation from herd, teeth grinding, restlessness = pain |

### CMT (California Mastitis Test) Procedure
1. Collect 2–3 mL of milk from each quarter into CMT paddle
2. Add equal volume CMT reagent (sodium lauryl sulphate solution)
3. Rotate gently for 10 seconds
4. **Reading:** No gel = Negative; Slight gel = Trace; Thick gel = Positive (treat)
5. **Record:** Date, animal tag, which quarter, result
6. **Test frequency:** Twice weekly in monsoon; once weekly rest of year; always test after mastitis treatment completion

## 13. REPRODUCTIVE MANAGEMENT

### Reproductive Targets — Murrah Herd

| Parameter | Target | Action if Below Target |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| First heat post-calving | 60–90 days | Check selenium/phosphorus status; body condition; call vet |
| First AI post-calving | 90–100 days | Wait for 2nd or 3rd natural heat for best conception |
| Conception rate (per AI) | ≥60% | If <50% for 3 months: check bull semen quality, AI timing, nutritional status |
| Pregnancy confirmation | 60–90 days post-AI | Rectal palpation by vet or progesterone milk test |
| Calving interval | ≤400 days | Review dry period, calving records; optimise AI timing |
| Dry period | 60 days (mandatory) | Shorter dry period reduces next lactation yield by 20–30% |
| Colostrum feeding to calf | First 2–3 days | 1/10 of calf bodyweight per day (≈3.5–4 L/day) |

### Estrus (Heat) Detection Signs
- Restless, increased activity, mounts other animals
- Stands to be mounted (standing heat — best time for AI)
- Mucus discharge from vulva (clear, stringy)
- Swollen, moist vulva
- Reduced milk yield for 1–2 days
- **Duration:** 12–24 hours in buffalo (shorter than cattle)
- **Best AI time:** 12–18 hours after onset of standing heat

### AI (Artificial Insemination) Programme
- Use proven Murrah bulls (EBV >500 kg milk daughter average)
- Semen available from: SVVU, Tirupati; LSGB Anantapur; BAIF Namdhari; Private AI centres
- Contact nearest AI technician: AP Animal Husbandry Dept, Gara Mandal
- **Synchronisation protocol (for batch breeding):** Ovsynch (GnRH + PGF2α) — discuss with vet

### Calving Management
| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| 3 weeks before calving | Move to separate calving pen; increase energy feed |
| 1 week before | Calcium drench (Calphosol) 300 mL orally daily |
| Day of calving | Monitor every 2 hours; assist only after 2 hrs of active labour |
| Within 30 min of calving | Clear calf airways; allow dam to lick calf |
| Within 2 hrs | Ensure calf receives colostrum (3–4 litres) |
| 6 hrs after calving | Oxytocin 20 IU IM to expel placenta; check for retained placenta |
| 24 hrs | Check uterine discharge (should be reddish-brown, not foul-smelling) |
| 72 hrs | IV Calcium if cow shows weakness |
| 14 days | Post-calving uterine check by vet; Oxytetracycline pessaries if discharge abnormal |

## 14. WATER QUALITY TESTING

### Parameters and Acceptable Ranges for Dairy Buffalo Drinking Water

| Parameter | Acceptable Range | Concern Threshold | Health Impact |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|---------------|
| **pH** | 6.5–8.5 | <6.0 or >9.0 | Digestive problems |
| **TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)** | <3,000 mg/L | >5,000 mg/L | Reduces intake, diarrhoea |
| **Nitrates (NO₃)** | <50 mg/L | >100 mg/L | Methaemoglobinaemia, abortions |
| **Fluoride** | <2.0 mg/L | >3.0 mg/L | Fluorosis, lameness — common in coastal AP |
| **Hardness (CaCO₃)** | <500 mg/L | >2,000 mg/L | Reduces Mg absorption |
| **Sulphates** | <500 mg/L | >1,000 mg/L | Diarrhoea, reduced mineral absorption |
| **Chlorides** | <600 mg/L | >1,500 mg/L | Off-taste; reduced intake |
| **E. coli / Coliforms** | Zero / 100 mL | Any positive | Diarrhoea, mastitis, reproductive failure |
| **Iron** | <0.3 mg/L | >1.0 mg/L | Bitter taste, reduced intake, oxidative damage |
| **Arsenic** | <0.05 mg/L | >0.1 mg/L | **Critical in coastal AP areas** |
| **Manganese** | <0.05 mg/L | >0.2 mg/L | Reproductive disorders |

### Water Requirement
- Lactating buffalo: **80–100 litres/day**
- Dry buffalo: 40–60 litres/day
- Shed cleaning: 20 litres/day/animal
- **Total for 40 animals: 3,500–4,500 litres/day (3.5–4.5 KL)**

### Srikakulam-Specific Water Concerns
> **Gara, Etcherla, Santhakaviti mandals:** Ground water often has elevated **fluoride (2–4 mg/L)** and **iron (>1 mg/L)**. If using borewell water, test BEFORE giving to animals.
>
> **Coastal mandals near sea:** Check for **chloride and TDS** — seawater intrusion in aquifers is common.
>
> **Post-flood:** Test immediately for coliforms, nitrates, and turbidity after any flood event.

### Water Testing Lab Recommendations

| Lab | Location | Tests | Cost |
|-----|----------|-------|------|
| Soil & Water Testing Lab, Dept. of Agriculture | Srikakulam town | pH, TDS, nitrates, fluoride, iron | Free / ₹50–200 |
| KVK Srikakulam | Srikakulam | Potable water panel | ₹100–300 |
| AP State Soil Health Lab | Rajam, Srikakulam | Full mineral panel | ₹300–500 |
| NABL-accredited private lab | Vizianagaram (40 km) | Full water quality + heavy metals | ₹800–2,000 |
| APPCB Environmental Lab | Visakhapatnam | Heavy metals, arsenic | ₹1,500–3,000 |

### Water Treatment at Farm
| Problem | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Bacteria / E. coli | Add sodium hypochlorite (bleaching powder) 0.5 ppm to water tank |
| High iron/turbidity | Aeration tank + sand filter |
| High fluoride | Activated alumina filter or nalgonda technique |
| High TDS | Reverse osmosis unit (if >5,000 mg/L TDS) |

### Water Testing Frequency
- **First time:** Before starting farm operations — full panel
- **Annual:** Pre-monsoon (April) — standard panel
- **After floods:** Immediately — coliforms + nitrates
- **If milk quality drops unexpectedly:** Test water first

## 15. SOIL TESTING FOR FODDER CULTIVATION

### Tests to Conduct Before Planting Napier/Maize

| Test | What it Measures | Target Range | Why Important |
|------|-----------------|-------------|---------------|
| **pH** | Soil acidity/alkalinity | 6.5–7.5 | Nutrient availability; below 6: add lime; above 7.5: add gypsum |
| **Organic Carbon (OC)** | Soil fertility indicator | >0.75% | <0.5% = add farmyard manure (FYM) 5 t/acre |
| **Available Nitrogen (N)** | Protein in fodder | Medium–High | Apply urea 50 kg/acre/cut |
| **Available Phosphorus (P₂O₅)** | Root development | 15–25 kg/ha | Apply SSP if deficient |
| **Available Potassium (K₂O)** | Disease resistance, yield | 120–180 kg/ha | Apply MOP if deficient |
| **Sulphur (S)** | Amino acid synthesis | >10 mg/kg | Apply gypsum if deficient |
| **Zinc (Zn)** | Micronutrient; fodder quality | >0.6 mg/kg | Apply zinc sulphate 10 kg/acre |
| **Boron (B)** | Legume fodder growth | >0.5 mg/kg | Apply borax if needed |
| **EC (Electrical Conductivity)** | Salinity level | <1.0 dS/m | >2 dS/m: leach soil before planting; don't plant Napier in saline soils |
